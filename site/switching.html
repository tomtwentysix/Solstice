Moving to Linux without starting over

If you've made records in Logic Pro, you've spent years learning where things are and what they're called. Solstice keeps that knowledge useful: the window is laid out the same way, the ideas carry the same names, and the single-key shortcuts you reach for without thinking do the same things. What's different is that it's free, it's open source, and it runs on the computer you already have.

Bringing your songs

Open a Logic Pro project bundle in Solstice and it reads the tempo, signature, key and sample rate, and places every recording at the bar where it was recorded, from the time stamp in each file. Passes recorded over the same spot come in as a take folder.

The arrangement is kept in an undocumented format, so for your edits and fades to come across as well, export Final Cut Pro XML from Logic Pro and save it next to the project. Solstice finds it and builds the session from it, and also picks up MIDI files and tracks exported to the Bounces folder. More on importing.

What's different

  • Plug-ins: Audio Units don't exist on Linux. Solstice hosts VST3, CLAP and LV2, and Windows VST3s through yabridge, which first-run setup helps you set up.
  • Built-in instruments: Solstice ships a synth, a sampler that imports SFZ, and a drum kit with a step sequencer, but not a library of sampled instruments. Bring your own samples or Linux instruments.
  • Audio: Solstice runs through PipeWire or JACK, the Linux equivalents of Core Audio for pro audio, and talks to one interface at a time.
  • The price: there isn't one, and there's no account to make.
