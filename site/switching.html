Switching

Switching to Solstice

Already know your way round a DAW? Solstice uses the layout, ideas and shortcuts that have become standard, on Linux, for free.

Moving to Linux without starting over

If you've made records in another DAW, you've spent years learning where things are and how a session fits together. Solstice is built around the layout and ideas that have become standard in professional recording software, so most of that knowledge carries straight across. What changes is that it's free, it's open source, and it runs on Linux.

Bringing your songs

Songs don't have to stay behind in the software they were started in. Solstice imports:

  • Audio files, several at once onto consecutive tracks, the way multitracks are usually handed over;
  • MIDI files, with their tempo map, signatures and markers;
  • Final Cut Pro XML, with clip positions, trims, gain and fades;
  • project bundles saved on a Mac, with the tempo, key and every recording placed where it was recorded.

What's different on Linux

  • Plug-ins: Linux uses VST3, CLAP and LV2. Windows VST3s run through yabridge, which first-run setup helps you set up. Mac-only Audio Units don't run on Linux.
  • Built-in instruments: Solstice ships a synth, a sampler that imports SFZ, and a drum kit with a step sequencer, but not a library of sampled instruments. Bring your own samples or Linux instruments.
  • Audio: Solstice runs through PipeWire or JACK, the Linux audio systems built for this kind of work, and talks to one interface at a time.
  • The price: there isn't one, and there's no account to make.
